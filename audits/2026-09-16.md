## Recommended actions

1. **Investigate the Gemini 429 exhaustion pattern.** The daily quota cap was hit in the very first run of the day (06:03–06:04 UTC), triggering fallback chains across all 7 subsequent runs. If this is recurring on high-volume days (105 golf signals suggests a large market universe), the fallback to flash-lite and then to cache is quietly degrading the research layer on busy days without any human-visible alarm. Check whether the Gemini free-tier quota has shrunk, whether the batch request size can be trimmed, or whether the paid tier should be activated. The result today — 0 Tavily credits, 14 fallbacks, 5/7 brief files errored — is a data-quality floor that should be surfaced to the operator before it becomes the new normal.

2. **Keep monitoring the ≥90% lay suppression; verify the floor is correctly enforced.** One ≥90% lay reached the tracker and resolved as a loss today. Given the established 1W/24L record in that band, it is worth confirming the suppression threshold (≤1.50 decimal odds, per the project's stated minimum lay odds) is actually gating all entries in this band at signal generation. A single instance is not evidence of a breach — it may be a probe-flagged trade logged intentionally — but after the 06-04 Gemini outage ran without fallback coverage, a belt-and-braces check of the suppression logic seems low-cost and appropriate.

## Caveats

**Resolutions (n=4):** A 25.0% WR and -£3.44 on 4 trades is noise. No meaningful inference about strategy performance is possible from this. You would need at minimum 30–50 same-category resolved trades to begin estimating a stable win rate with confidence intervals narrower than ±15pp.

**Signal counts (n=121):** The 3-lay / 118-back split and 105-golf dominance are real structural numbers, but they reflect a single day of market availability — September is a calendar-transition period (post-golf-summer, pre-football-season) and the sport mix may look different in a week. The structural back-bias finding is robust across many prior days; today's data is consistent with it but does not independently confirm it.

**Gemini fallback chain:** It is possible some of the 119 "markets with errors" were partially recovered by the fallback sequence (flash-lite, then cache). The audit data does not distinguish between a brief that came entirely from an errored primary call and one partially supplemented by fallback. The 0 Tavily credits figure is the clearest indicator that no fresh external research ran today.

**CLV staleness:** The CLV sidecar was last generated 2026-09-02. Without current CLV data, there is no way to assess whether today's signals (especially the 10–11pp edge-band majority, n=80) were beating or trailing the closing price. This gap in the audit trail is structural until the CLV generation is brought back up to date.
