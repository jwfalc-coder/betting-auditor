# Research Audit — 2026-09-10

## Summary

A quiet, mechanically healthy day: all 7 runs completed with clean exits, no tracebacks, and no contamination flags. 22 signals were generated but only 5 reached the Telegram slip, with the day's resolved trades (12) producing a 41.7% win rate and -£2.70 net — a mild loss driven entirely by the lay side going 0/2. The day's lay sample (n=3 generated, n=2 resolved) is far too small to move any standing conclusion, but the back-dominated signal mix and degraded primary research path are worth noting.

## Observations

1. **Signal mix is 86% backs, with only 1 lay in the historically-supported probability band.** Of 22 signals generated, 19 were backs and 3 were lays. Of the 3 lays, 2 fell in the 65–89.99% market-probability band and only 1 fell in the <65% band — the sole zone identified as historically profitable. 20 of 22 signals carried `back_probe=true` (tracking-only), so the live-placeable set was tiny. Confidence: speculative (n=22 total, n=3 lays). This is consistent with the known structural back-bias of the keyword model and the documented lay drought; it neither confirms nor challenges established finding #1, since the <65% lay sample is n=1.

2. **Both resolved lays lost; the <65% band lay lost -£2.00.** The day's 2 resolved lays went 0/2 (0% WR, -£2.80 combined). Broken down: the single <65% lay lost -£2.00; the single 65–89.99% lay lost -£0.80. The <65% result is a single data point against established finding #1 ("lays below 65% are the only proven edge"), but n=1 is not evidence of anything — a single loss is exactly what the historical win rate predicts will happen roughly 30–40% of the time even in a well-calibrated model. Confidence: speculative (n=2 resolved lays). No action warranted; sample is uninformative.

3. **Primary research path degraded: 26 Gemini 429s, 14 fallback activations, 58% cache rate, 0 Tavily credits.** The Gemini daily cap was hit repeatedly across the day (26 instances), triggering 14 research fallback events. 219 of 378 markets (57.9%) were served from cache rather than fresh research. Zero Tavily credits were used, meaning all live research came from Gemini (where available) or prior cache. This is directly relevant to established finding #4: if the primary research step was constrained for much of the day — with over half of results from cache and the Gemini quota exhausted early — the already-negligible ~0.13pp research contribution to edge is likely lower still on days like this. However, since finding #4 says the measured contribution is near-zero regardless, the practical signal-quality impact is probably small. The pipeline health concern is different: consistent daily-cap exhaustion suggests the run schedule is outpacing the Gemini quota, which may cause silent quality degradation that's hard to detect from win-rate data alone. Confidence: medium on the pattern (7 clean runs, consistent 429s).

4. **Overall resolved win rate 41.7% (5/12), with backs at 50% WR and essentially flat (+£0.10).** The 12 resolved trades produced a 41.7% win rate and -£2.70 P&L. Stripping out the 2 lays, the 10 resolved backs went 5/5-loss = 50% WR and +£0.10 — flat by any reasonable read. This is unremarkable for a flat-£2-stake tracking-only probe: 50% is consistent with the back-probe hypothesis that most sports' back-side edges are unproven but not obviously negative. It does not support or undermine established finding #3 (model calibration indistinguishable from market pricing) — at n=10, the expected range of outcomes is far too wide to distinguish a 50% outcome from a 45% or 55% true rate. Confidence: speculative (n=12 total).

5. **CLV tracking appears stale: 0 entries logged for 2026-09-10, with the last CLV file generated 2026-09-02 (8 days prior).** The CLV sidecar shows `generated_at: 2026-09-02T18:06:17` and reports 0 headline-eligible entries for 2026-09-10. This is a measurement gap: closing-line value is the most model-independent way to assess whether entry prices are better or worse than the market's eventual assessment, and if the CLV mechanism has been silent for 8 days it provides no signal on recent trades. This does not affect today's P&L figures but it does mean a useful diagnostic tool is not currently functioning.

## Recommended actions

1. **Investigate the stale CLV sidecar.** The `clv_reconstructed.json` file was last generated 2026-09-02 and logged 0 entries for the full 8-day gap since. Given that CLV is the cleanest way to audit entry price quality independently of win/loss luck, it is worth establishing whether `price_capture.py` (the `:15/:45 cron`) is running and writing data, whether the CLV reconstruction job is consuming it, or whether the issue is earlier in the chain. Check `data/csv_logs/` and the `:15/:45` cron job logs before the next session; fix is likely a single broken cron entry or a path mismatch. No changes to trading logic needed.

2. **Keep monitoring the Gemini 429 rate; no threshold change yet.** 26 daily-cap hits across 7 runs is a consistent signal that the research pipeline is hitting quota limits regularly. Given finding #4 (research contributes ~0.13pp of edge), the direct P&L effect is likely negligible — but if the fallback chain is silently producing lower-quality signals for a large fraction of markets, it could contaminate the probe-segment win-rate data being collected to inform future strategy decisions. Monitor the 429 count over the next 5–7 days; if it remains ≥20/day, consider whether the run schedule or Gemini quota tier should be adjusted. No action today — one day's pattern is not sufficient to justify a change.

## Caveats

All resolved-trade figures (n=12 total, n=2 lays, n=10 backs) are single-day counts. At these sample sizes, every win-rate figure has a 95% confidence interval spanning roughly ±25–30 percentage points. A 0% lay win rate on 2 trades is entirely consistent with a true win rate of 40–60%; a 50% back win rate on 10 trades is consistent with a true rate of 20–80%. No number cited here should be treated as a trend, and none of the observations above should change any live threshold or strategy parameter on this day's data alone. The 429/fallback pipeline observation is the most actionable because it is a factual count over 7 runs (not a statistical inference), but even there the downstream impact on signal quality is uncertain and would need multi-week data to quantify. The established finding #1 (<65% lays as the only proven edge) is based on the full historical trade history; today's n=1 result in that band is a single data point that neither confirms nor undermines it.
