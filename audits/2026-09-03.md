## Observations

1. **All signals — including lays — are back_probe=true.** Every one of the 38 signals generated on 2026-09-03 carries `back_probe=true`. The field notes explicitly flag this pattern as non-trivial: it could indicate a project-wide probe-hold switch is still active, or that some other mechanism is forcing lay signals into tracking-only mode. The consequence is material: 7 slip notifications went to the operator, but not one signal on any of them was actually placeable. The root cause — whether a config flag that outlived its stated revert date, a code path applying back-probe logic too broadly, or something else — is not determinable from this data alone and should be confirmed in the codebase directly. Confidence: high (the count is 38/38 with no exceptions). Speculative on root cause.

2. **Lay signals cluster in the unproven 65–89.99% market-probability band.** Of the 6 lay signals generated, 5 fall in the 65–89.99% implied-probability band and only 1 falls below 65%. Per established finding #1 — that the only demonstrated edge in this bot's trade history is lays on selections below the 65% threshold — today's lay generation is mis-weighted toward the unproven segment. The single resolved lay trade (-£1.44, 0 wins) also lands in the 65–89.99% band, which is consistent with that band lacking edge, though n=1 is trivial and proves nothing on its own. Confidence: speculative (n=6 lay signals, n=1 lay resolution). The signal-generation weighting concern is real regardless of sample size; the P&L point is not.

3. **Darts and Australian Rules constitute 44.7% of all signals with no visible calibration baseline.** Darts (7 signals) and Australian rules (10 signals) together account for 17 of 38 signals — more than any other pair of sports. These sports do not appear in the project's established calibration history (which is golf-, politics-, and soccer-heavy). Applying the same keyword model and calibration bands to sports with no validated resolved-trade base carries unknown directional risk. This is not necessarily a bug, but it is a gap: if these markets are driving signal volume, their segment-level win rate should be tracked explicitly before treating their signals as comparable to established categories. Confidence: speculative (n=17 signals, no resolution data for these sports visible in today's data).

4. **Gemini 429 rate-limiting is structural across all runs.** 22 rate-limit errors and 14 research fallbacks across 8 runs averages roughly 1.75 fallbacks per routine. Per established finding #4, web research contributes only ~0.13 percentage points of a signal's stated edge on average, but the 429 errors matter for a different reason: when the primary provider hits its daily cap, the fallback path is a different model with potentially different grounding behaviour. If phantom-edge contamination (BUG-151, BUG-296) is associated with specific provider output formats, fallback-path output is an additional vector for that contamination, and 14 activations in one day is enough to affect a meaningful fraction of the day's 351 researched markets. The 0 Tavily credits logged also means all research was Gemini- or cache-sourced; Tavily does not appear to have been called at all on this date. Confidence: medium on the structural rate-limiting observation; speculative on the contamination-via-fallback inference.

5. **BUG-151 result-contamination continues on cricket County Championship markets.** 6 contamination warnings were logged, all on cricket County Championship Division One and Two matches where `key_facts` contained live scores and match dates. This is a known, previously-flagged bug. The fact that it is still firing at the same rate as prior audits suggests the fix either has not been deployed to the affected code path or is not catching all variants of the contamination pattern. Today's contaminated markets (Essex v Sussex, Somerset v Glamorgan, Northamptonshire v Durham) are multi-day matches, which is the structural reason the contamination recurs — the research pipeline ingests in-progress match scores as if they were predictive facts. No cricket signals appear in today's lay signal list, so the direct P&L impact today is not visible, but the contamination source is still live. Confidence: high (6 explicit WARNING log lines cited directly).

## Recommended actions

1. **Confirm why all signals — including lays — are back_probe=true, and resolve if it is a stale config flag.** If a project-wide probe-hold switch was set with a stated revert date that has now passed, and it is still active on 2026-09-03, every slip notification since that date has been a non-actionable ghost. Verify the current state of the relevant config flag and the code path that applies `back_probe` to lay signals, and update accordingly. This is the highest-priority action from today's data.

2. **Open a tracking segment for Darts and Australian Rules with an explicit n≥30 gate before any promotion decision.** Both sports are generating signals at meaningful volume (17/38 today) with no visible calibration history. Add them to whatever segment-milestone system the project uses for the back-probe review cycle, so the model's performance on these sports is measured before assumptions from golf or soccer are extended to them. No live behaviour change is needed now — just ensure the resolved-trade data is captured by sport so a future review can use it.

## Caveats

All P&L and win-rate figures from today's resolutions (n=4) are statistically meaningless at face value — a 50% WR from 4 trades could arise by chance from almost any underlying distribution. The lay win-rate observation (0/1) and the back win-rate (2/3) should not update any priors. The signal-generation patterns (n=38 signals, n=6 lays) are large enough to comment on structurally — the back/lay imbalance and the market-probability-band skew are real observations about what the model produced today — but a single day cannot confirm or deny any of the four established findings. Confirming or contradicting finding #1 (lays below 65% as the sole proven edge) would require accumulating resolved lay trades by band; today adds n=1 to the 65–89.99% band and n=0 to the below-65% band. The Gemini 429 and contamination observations are repeatable across the full run history and carry more weight than the P&L numbers, but root-cause inference from log counts alone remains speculative.
