# Research Audit — 2026-09-08

## Summary

All 7 scheduled pipeline runs completed with clean exits and produced signals, so mechanically the day was unremarkable. The more substantive finding is that every single one of the day's 27 signals — including the sole lay signal — carries `back_probe=true`, meaning nothing generated today was ever eligible for manual placement. Combined with a 26-back / 1-lay output ratio and Gemini being exhausted entirely across the last three runs, the day raises one specific operational question worth checking immediately: whether the project-wide probe hold (MOD-335) is still active in config, five weeks past its intended expiry.

## Observations

1. **All 27 signals are tracking-only, including the only lay.** `back_probe_signals = 27` equals `signals_total = 27`, and that includes the single lay recommendation. The field-note accompanying this data explicitly flags this as worth surfacing rather than treating as a labelling artefact. There are two candidate explanations: (a) the project-wide `global_probe_hold` flag introduced for the operator's absence (documented as a ~2026-07-29 to ~2026-08-05/06 temporary measure) was never reverted and is still forcing every signal — including lays — into tracking-only mode; or (b) the probe mechanism is incorrectly tagging lay signals in a way that was not intended. If explanation (a) is correct, every lay that should have been placeable since approximately early August has been silently blocked. This cannot be resolved from the data alone, but it is the most operationally consequential question the day raises. Confidence: medium (mechanism is clear; which explanation applies requires a config check).

2. **The model generated 26 back signals and 1 lay — a 96%/4% ratio.** This continues the structural back-bias documented historically (prior audit sessions noted that ~92% of signals carry zero lay-boost from the keyword model). Today's single lay, in the `>=90%` market-implied-probability band, is exactly the segment identified by established finding #2 as the worst-performing lay band (historically 1W/24L). The back-probe mechanism is catching all 26 backs correctly as tracking-only, which is the right operational outcome; but the 96/4 ratio itself is a reminder that the keyword model is not organically producing lay signals in the way the stated lay-only strategy would ideally require. Confidence: high (the count is from 7 separate research files). Relation to established findings #2 and #3: consistent with both — the model's output distribution has not shifted toward lays, and the one lay it did generate is in the segment with documented negative EV.

3. **Gemini was fully exhausted and disabled in 3 of the last 4 runs (15:00, 18:00, 20:00).** The error log shows 35 rate-limit 429s, 44 503s, 14 research-fallback activations, and 4 `all models exhausted/failed` events — the latter all clustered in the afternoon and evening. The 06:00 run hit 429s on `gemini-2.5-flash` repeatedly before even the first fallback completed. This means signals generated in the 15:00, 18:00, and 20:00 runs were produced without any Gemini contribution at all; the research assessment for those windows relied entirely on the fallback path. Given established finding #4 (web research contributes ~0.13pp of a signal's stated edge), the practical effect on signal quality is probably small — but the daily API ceiling is clearly being hit by the early afternoon, which is worth noting for capacity planning. Confidence: high (errors are concrete log lines, not inferred).

4. **Zero Tavily credits were consumed across 378 researched markets (217 cache hits).** Every market researched today drew on cached or non-Tavily sources; no fresh Tavily search was run. This is directly consistent with established finding #4 — if live Tavily research contributes ~0.13pp of edge on average, days of pure-cache research should produce signals statistically indistinguishable from days with live search. The number is worth tracking over time: if `total_tavily_credits = 0` becomes the norm rather than the exception, it implies the research step's already-minimal real information input has dropped to zero. Confidence: high (the count is explicit). Sample caveat: this is one day; cache-hit rate depends on market overlap with prior runs, so a single day tells us nothing about the trend.

5. **3 resolutions, all lays, 33.3% win rate, -£0.46 P&L; the >=90% band went 0/1.** The sole `>=90%` market-implied-probability lay lost, adding one more data point to the historical 1W/24L count in that band (established finding #2). The 65–89.99% band went 1/2 (50.0% WR, -£0.22). Neither band has enough volume today (n=1 and n=2 respectively) to move any historical conclusion. No CLV data was logged for this day (`n_headline_eligible = 0`), and the CLV sidecar itself had not been regenerated since 2026-09-02. Confidence: speculative (n=3 total; n=1 in the most interesting band). These numbers are noted for ledger continuity, not as evidence of anything.

## Recommended actions

1. **Check whether `global_probe_hold` is still set to `true` in `config/config.toml`.** The intended revert window was ~2026-08-05/06; today is 2026-09-08. If the flag is still on, it has been silently blocking all lay placements for approximately five weeks. This is a one-line config check (`grep global_probe_hold config/config.toml`) and should be done before the next routine fires. If it is off, investigate why `back_probe=true` is appearing on lay signals — that is the other candidate explanation and equally worth resolving.

2. **Keep monitoring Gemini exhaustion timing.** Three consecutive afternoon/evening runs ran without Gemini today. If this pattern repeats across multiple days, consider whether the daily cap can be raised, whether the run schedule can be adjusted to spread API load, or whether the fallback path needs to be treated as the de-facto primary for afternoon runs. No action is warranted from a single day, but if the 15:00 run consistently triggers `provider_exhausted`, the downstream signal quality (however marginal) for the heaviest-volume part of the day is reliably degraded.

## Caveats

All resolution figures (n=3) are far too small to support any statistical claim. A 33.3% win rate on 3 trades is consistent with both a very good and a very bad strategy; the binomial confidence interval at this n is effectively the entire probability range. The `>=90%` lay band observation (0/1 today) adds one count to an existing historical series but does not move the 1W/24L conclusion materially either way — that series would need dozens more resolved trades to meaningfully update. The 26-back / 1-lay ratio is informative about the model's output distribution but tells us nothing about whether those backs or that lay would be profitable; the back-probe mechanism prevents any live resolution from testing that directly. The Gemini exhaustion finding is mechanically certain (concrete log lines) but its effect on signal quality is bounded by the ~0.13pp average research contribution documented in established finding #4 — the practical ceiling on harm here is very low. No CLV data is available for this day, so there is no independent price-anchor check on any of the resolutions.
