# Research Audit — 2026-09-23

## Summary

An unremarkable day operationally — all 7 scheduled runs completed with clean exits, no tracebacks — but three things are worth examining: the signal generation continues to produce almost exclusively backs (33/35), with lay generation so thin that the two lay signals that did appear skew toward a problematic band; the Gemini research provider was exhausted before the first run finished, with 14 fallback activations across the day; and the CLV sidecar is 21 days stale, meaning there is currently no ability to measure whether recent signals beat the closing line. Five back-side resolutions (60.0% WR, £2.05) are too few to move any calibration finding in either direction.

## Observations

1. **Signal side ratio is 33 backs to 2 lays — structural back-lean is unchanged.** Of 35 total signals generated across the day, 33 were BACK and only 2 were LAY. 34 of 35 signals carry `back_probe=true` (tracking-only, not placeable), meaning effectively one signal reached slip-eligible status across the entire day. The back-dominance is consistent with the project's known structural issue: the keyword model generates almost no organic lay signals. *Established finding #1 (lay-side is the only proven edge) is neither confirmed nor contradicted here — today's data is simply insufficient, with 2 lays generated — but the low lay count is itself a standing concern: if the model's only proven edge is lays, a day generating 2 lays from 35 signals is not a day generating meaningful alpha.* Confidence: medium (this pattern is consistent across many prior sessions, not just today).

2. **One of the two lay signals sits in the ≥90% market-implied probability band — the known 1W/24L segment.** The `by_lay_market_prob_band` breakdown shows 1 lay in `<65%` (the historically supported band per established finding #1) and 1 in `≥90%` (the segment with an established 1W/24L live record). The min-lay-odds floor (1.50 decimal) should suppress ≥90%-implied lays as placeable trades — and with 34/35 signals marked `back_probe=true`, this is almost certainly a probe/tracking-only log. That is by design. However, accumulating probe data in a band already carrying a 4% historical win rate adds data to a question that is arguably already answered. Confidence: speculative (n=1 lay in that band today; the underlying 1W/24L figure is from the broader live history, not today's data).

3. **Gemini was exhausted before 06:05 UTC; 14 fallback activations fired across the day.** The error log shows 29 rate-limit 429s and 14 `research_fallback_activated` entries, with one explicit `all models exhausted/failed — disabling Gemini for this run` at 06:06. The 06:00 UTC run — the first of seven — lost its primary research provider entirely. This matters because research fallback degrades the quality of `key_facts` input to the model. *Established finding #4 says real research contributes ~0.13pp of edge on average, so degraded research may not materially change signal quality — but it does mean the model is running on cached or fallback-sourced content for a large fraction of markets.* 250 of 378 researched markets (66.1%) were served from cache today; genuine fresh web research was the minority path. Confidence: high on the factual pipeline degradation; speculative on its signal-quality impact given finding #4.

4. **One result contamination flag fired (BUG-151) — score leakage on England v Sri Lanka.** A single `[WARNING] Result contamination detected (BUG-151)` entry appeared in the 22:14 run for England v Sri Lanka, with score information ("3-0 T20I sweep") present in a `key_fact`. This is a known, logged bug with an existing detection mechanism. One instance in seven runs is not alarming, but it confirms the contamination path is still live and firing on cricket markets. Confidence: high (direct log quote).

5. **CLV data is 21 days stale (last generated 2026-09-02); zero CLV entries logged for 2026-09-23.** The closing-line value sidecar reports `generated_at: 2026-09-02T18:06:17` with `n_logged_that_day: 0` and `n_headline_eligible: 0`. CLV is how this project measures whether signals beat the market close — i.e., whether the prices taken were positive-expectation relative to the efficient market. With the sidecar unrefreshed for three weeks, there is currently no measurement of CLV for any recent trade. This is an infrastructure gap, not a signal-quality finding, but it means one of the key diagnostic instruments is dark. Confidence: high on the staleness; speculative on what CLV would show if refreshed.

## Recommended actions

1. **Investigate whether the ≥90%-implied-probability lay probe is worth continuing to log.** The established 1W/24L result in that band is not a suggestive trend — it is a large enough sample to constitute a reasonable prior that the band is losing. Probe data in an already-condemned band consumes tracking slots without a clear hypothesis it is testing. Review whether the probe-hold mechanism can be extended to exclude ≥90% lays from logging entirely, or whether there is a specific sub-segment (e.g., a sport or market type) that justifies continued data collection. No live-strategy change is implied — this is tracking-log hygiene only.

2. **Restore the CLV sidecar update process before the next reconciliation.** The sidecar has been stale since 2026-09-02 — 21 days. Without it, there is no independent measure of whether signals are beating the closing line. This is especially important if the operator is weighing whether to re-enable Kelly sizing or expand placed-trade segments; those decisions benefit from CLV evidence. Run `scripts/price_capture.py` or whichever script updates `clv_reconstructed.json` and verify it logs entries for currently-open positions before the next routine cycle.

## Caveats

Five resolutions on 2026-09-23 (all backs, 60.0% WR) is n=5 — well below the n≥15 threshold for drawing any statistical inference. The 60.0% WR is consistent with both a healthy back-side and a mildly unlucky one; it means nothing on its own. The 2 lay signals observed today cannot test any band-level lay hypothesis (n=1 per band). Any observation in this report labelled "speculative" requires at minimum several weeks of consistent daily data before it could justify a threshold change. The one exception is the CLV sidecar staleness, which is a factual infrastructure observation requiring no statistical threshold to act on.
