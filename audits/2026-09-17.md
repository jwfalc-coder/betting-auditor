# Research Audit — 2026-09-17

## Summary

Pipeline ran cleanly (7/7 runs, zero tracebacks), but two anomalies warrant investigation: every one of the day's 85 signals — including the 2 lay signals — carries `back_probe=true`, suggesting the global probe hold may still be active well past its expected revert window, and zero Tavily credits were consumed across all 378 researched markets (pure cache day). Only 2 resolutions settled (n too small to draw conclusions). Six signals reached the Telegram slip, but if the probe hold is still live, none were placeable.

## Observations

1. **All 85 signals are probe-flagged, including the 2 lay signals.** `back_probe_signals = 85`, against a total of 85 signals. The field notes flag this pattern explicitly: if `back_probe=true` appears on effectively all signals including lays, it is consistent with the project-wide `global_probe_hold` switch being active. That switch was noted as temporary (target revert ~2026-08-05/06); if it remains on as of 2026-09-17, no signal has been placeable for roughly six consecutive weeks. Six signals did reach the slip across three runs (1800, 2000, 2200), but under a live probe hold all six would be tracking-only and should show a "do NOT place" annotation. Confidence: medium (the flag pattern is unambiguous; the cause — probe hold vs. something else forcing back_probe — requires a config check to confirm). *Directly relevant to established finding #1: the model's only demonstrated edge is lay-side, but a hold that suppresses all placement also suppresses all lay execution.*

2. **Zero fresh research credits were used; the entire day ran on cached briefs.** `total_tavily_credits = 0`, `cache_hits = 218` out of 378 markets researched (50 with errors, 22 deferred). This is partly explained by the 36 Gemini 429s (daily cap hit in the 06:00 run) and 14 research fallback activations. Confidence: high (the credit count is a direct counter). *Consistent with established finding #4 (~0.13pp average research contribution to stated edge): if research contributes almost nothing, running on cached data is unlikely to materially change signal quality. However, it is worth noting that 50/378 markets errored and 22 were deferred — meaning roughly 19% of scanned markets received no analysis at all today, not just stale analysis.*

3. **Structural back-bias: 83 backs vs. 2 lays (2.4% lay rate).** Of 85 total signals, only 2 are lay recommendations, both falling in the 65–89.99% market-implied probability band. Zero lays were generated in the ≥90% band (the 1W/24L segment identified in established finding #2). Confidence: high for the count; speculative for any inference, since 85 is a single-day sample. *This is consistent with the long-running observation that the model is structurally back-biased and produces very few organic lay signals — but the near-zero lay rate also means lay-side performance data accumulates slowly, making it hard to validate or falsify established finding #1 at pace.*

4. **Golf accounts for 59/85 signals (69.4%).** The next largest category is Australian Rules at 8 (9.4%), followed by cricket at 6 (7.1%), rugby union and baseball at 3 each, soccer and darts at 2 each, and politics and snooker at 1 each. All edge falls in the 7–11pp range (43 signals at 10–11pp, 40 at 7–9pp, 2 at 12pp+). Confidence: high for the counts. Golf's dominance is a persistent structural feature of the model, not a one-day artefact — but a day where golf season is active will naturally inflate these numbers, so calendar context matters.

5. **Two resolutions, n far too small to assess.** One back settled as a win (+£2.77), one lay settled as a loss (−£0.28), net +£2.49 on the day. The lay loss was in the 65–89.99% market-prob band — the same band as both of today's generated lay signals. A single resolved lay in this band tells us nothing about whether that band is profitable; the established finding on the ≥90% segment (1W/24L) is not touched by today's data. CLV data was available but had zero entries logged that day, and the CLV file itself was generated on 2026-09-02 (17 days stale) — no CLV inference is possible. Confidence: low/speculative on all resolution-derived numbers.

## Recommended actions

1. **Confirm whether `global_probe_hold` is still set to `true` in config.** The pattern of 100% probe-flagged signals — including lay signals — is the clearest single thing this data raises. If the hold is still on, the operator should make a deliberate decision: either revert it (as was originally intended by ~Aug 5–6) or formally extend it with a new target date. A hold that has been silently active for six weeks without a logged decision is an unacknowledged operational state. Action: read `config/config.toml` → `thresholds.global_probe_hold`; if `true`, update `modifications.md` with the current rationale or revert.

2. **Keep monitoring Gemini API capacity.** Thirty-six 429s and 14 fallback activations in a single day, with the first brief erroring entirely, suggests the Gemini free-tier daily cap is being hit in the early-morning run and cascading through the day. This is not a new problem (fallback logic exists for it), but if the fallback is covering 0/25 groups (as one sample line shows), some research is silently absent rather than degraded. No threshold change is warranted from one day's data alone — but if this pattern persists across the next 5–7 days' audits, the 06:00 run's research volume relative to the daily cap is worth reviewing.

## Caveats

All resolution-derived statistics (win rate, P&L, lay band performance) are from n=2 settled trades. This is a noise floor, not a data point: a 50% WR on 2 trades is meaningless, and the single lay loss in the 65–89.99% band adds nothing to established finding #1 or #2. A minimum of n=30 resolved trades per segment is the threshold noted in the project for promotion or kill decisions; n=2 doesn't move any segment. Similarly, the 85 generated signals are a one-day count — the back/lay ratio and golf concentration are consistent with known structural features of the model, but a single active golf tournament can inflate golf counts by 30–40 signals on its own. The most actionable item in this audit (the probe-hold status) does not depend on sample size — it is a yes/no config question.
