Confidence: medium (n=86 is reasonable for a single day's output, but this pattern should be confirmed over multiple days before drawing conclusions about model drift). *Engages established finding #1.*

2. **98.8% of signals are probe/tracking-only (85 of 86).** The `back_probe` flag marks a signal as never-to-be-placed. The field notes explicitly flag this as a checkable pattern rather than a labelling artefact: it is consistent with a project-wide probe hold being active, but should be verified — if this is unintentional, a full day of potential live signals has been silently swallowed. Confidence: speculative (single day; intent cannot be confirmed from signal data alone — requires checking the probe-hold flag in config).

Confidence: speculative (n=2 lays total — directionally consistent with known findings, but no statistical weight). *Directly engages established finding #2.*

## Recommended actions

1. **Confirm whether the near-total probe hold is intentional.** 85/86 signals being tracking-only for a full day means the operator received at most 1 potentially-placeable signal across 7 runs. If this is a deliberate global probe hold, no action needed — but the audit log should say so. If it is not intentional, a misconfigured probe flag is silently suppressing live operation. Check the current value of the project-wide probe hold switch and, if active, note the expected end date; if inactive, investigate why 83+ of 84 back signals and possibly 1 of 2 lay signals are still marked probe.

2. **Log the Gemini 429 frequency over the past 7 days and assess whether the daily cap is being consumed within the first run.** The samples show the cap hit at 06:04 on the first run. If the cap is exhausted that early consistently, every subsequent run that day falls back to secondary providers — or runs uncovered. Given finding #4 (research contribution ~0.13pp), the signal-quality impact is probably small, but uncovered briefs (the 3 groups not reached by fallback on the 06:00 run) represent a silent data gap. A simple grep over recent logs for "daily cap" timestamps would establish whether this is a new or longstanding pattern.

## Caveats

All resolution figures (n=9, wins=2, WR=22.2%, P&L=-£8.16) represent a single calendar day and carry no statistical weight individually. Any win-rate read from n<15 is noise — even an 0-for-8 back stretch (as seen here) has roughly a 9% probability of occurring by chance at a true 70% win rate. The lay result (n=1, 100% WR, +£1.90) is a single data point and cannot be used to confirm or update the lay edge finding in any direction.

The probe/tracking-only signal count (85/86) cannot be fully interpreted without knowing whether the project-wide probe hold is active. If it is, the near-zero live output is intentional and none of the day's signal counts reflect real-time strategy execution.

The CLV sidecar was last generated 2026-09-02 (not refreshed on the target date) and logged zero headline-eligible entries for 2026-09-18. CLV analysis therefore adds nothing to this day's audit.

For any of the day's numbers to support a strategy change, the relevant segment would need n≥30 resolved trades at minimum, and ideally n≥50 with consistent conditions (same probe-hold state, same provider mix) across all of them.
