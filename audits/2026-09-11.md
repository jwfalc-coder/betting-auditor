# Research Audit — 2026-09-11

## Summary

All seven scheduled pipeline runs completed with clean exits, and no tracebacks or hard errors were logged — a healthy operational day by that measure. However, every one of the 28 signals generated was flagged `back_probe=true`, meaning nothing reached the operator as a live placeable bet; the slip sent only 3 signals across the whole day (2 at 06:00, 1 at 22:00) and all were tracking-only. Resolutions were a bad result — 11.1% win rate on 9 settled trades, -£10.38 — but the sample is far too small to draw conclusions from. The more structurally interesting finding is that the probe-hold flag appears to still be live, and the model's raw output remains overwhelmingly back-skewed even with a lay-only strategy nominally in place.

## Observations

1. **All 28 generated signals, including lays, are flagged tracking-only.** `back_probe_signals = 28`, which equals `signals_total`. The field notes for this audit explicitly flag this as a checkable pattern rather than a labelling artefact: if lays are also probe-flagged, a project-wide probe hold (`global_probe_hold = true`) is the most plausible explanation. The intended revert window was approximately 2026-08-05/06 — roughly six weeks before this audit date. Either the flag was deliberately kept on beyond that window, or it was never reverted. Confidence: **medium** (the data is unambiguous; the cause requires a config read to confirm).

2. **The model's organic output is 89% backs (25/28 signals), with only 3 lays.** Engaging directly with established finding #1 — that the only demonstrated edge is lays below 65% implied probability — the raw signal composition here is near-inverted from that conclusion. Of the 3 lay signals, only 1 fell in the proven <65% band; the other 2 fell in the >=90% band (the 1W/24L segment flagged in established finding #2). The lay-only strategy is a downstream filter suppressing the model's organic output, not a property of the model itself. This is consistent with previously documented structural back-bias (the model boosts favourites in approximately 92% of cases by zero, and rarely generates a genuine negative). Confidence: **medium** (n=28 is small for a single day, but the directional finding is consistent with the pre-existing documented bias, not a one-day anomaly).

3. **Research ran entirely from cache — zero Tavily credits consumed.** 378 markets were researched, 250 cache hits recorded, and 0 live Tavily queries were made. Engaging with established finding #4 (~0.13 percentage points average research contribution to stated edge): if the research step genuinely contributes almost nothing to signal quality, cache-only operation may be operationally harmless. However, the reason for zero live queries is not explained in this data — it could be deliberate throttling, a silent API failure, or simply that all queried markets had valid cached briefs. Two of the seven brief files had `status: error`, which may be related. Worth distinguishing between "cache is working as designed" and "live queries are silently not firing." Confidence: **speculative** (the data shows the output, not the cause).

4. **30 Gemini 429 errors and 14 research fallbacks in a single day, concentrated in the 06:00 run.** The 06:00 run alone triggered 23 fallback activations (all covered by the Claude Code fallback path). The daily API cap was evidently hit within the first run of the day, leaving six subsequent runs to absorb whatever quota remained. Three 503 transient errors also appeared. No provider was fully exhausted and no tracebacks followed, so recovery worked as designed — but a consistent daily-cap breach at the first run suggests the 06:00 budget allocation may be too aggressive relative to total daily quota. Confidence: **medium** (the pattern is clear in the logs; whether it recurs daily would require more than one day's data).

5. **Nine resolutions: 11.1% win rate, -£10.38.** Backs went 1/7 (14.3% WR, -£10.10); lays went 0/2 (0.0% WR, -£0.28). The two resolved lays were both in the 65-89.99% band — not the <65% proven band (established finding #1) and not the >=90% disaster band (established finding #2). No resolution data exists for either of those bands today. The consecutive-losses counter reached 3 by the 18:00 run and held there through close. Confidence: **speculative** — n=9 is far too small to draw any directional inference; a 69% historical win rate produces runs like this by chance, and a single-day 11.1% WR should be treated as variance, not signal, unless the pattern persists across weeks.

## Recommended actions

1. **Check whether `global_probe_hold` is still set in config.** Every signal today — including lays — was forced tracking-only, six weeks after the intended revert date. If this is an unintentional omission, reverting the flag would immediately re-enable live lay placement. If it was deliberately extended, that decision should be documented explicitly rather than left as an invisible config state. This is the highest-priority action from today's audit because it determines whether the bot is producing any operational output at all.

2. **Investigate the zero-Tavily-credit run before assuming cache-only behaviour is benign.** Two brief files returned `status: error`, and no live queries fired despite 378 markets being processed. Given that established finding #4 already places research contribution close to zero, this may matter little for signal quality — but if live queries are silently failing (as opposed to cache being intentionally preferred), that failure mode should be confirmed and documented. No live action is warranted until the cause is established; this is a monitoring item.

## Caveats

All resolution figures (n=9) are drawn from a single calendar day. At this sample size, a 11.1% win rate is statistically compatible with a true rate anywhere from roughly 1% to 48% (95% CI on a Bernoulli process). No conclusions about strategy performance should be updated on this number. The lay sub-sample (n=2) is similarly uninformative. For any band-level inference to be actionable, a minimum of n≥30 resolved trades in that specific band would be needed, and ideally n≥50 to distinguish a genuine edge shift from variance. The established findings cited throughout this audit are based on cumulative history well above that threshold; a single day's data can be in tension with them without overturning them.

The CLV sidecar was last generated 2026-09-02, and no headline-eligible CLV entries were logged on 2026-09-11. CLV analysis is therefore unavailable for this date and has been omitted rather than estimated.
