# Research Audit — 2026-09-02

A quiet day operationally: all 7 scheduled pipeline runs completed with clean exits and no tracebacks. Signal generation was heavily skewed back (35 of 37 total signals), with only 2 lay signals produced across the entire day — one of which sits in the ≥90% implied-probability band, the known worst-performing segment. ## Observations

1. **The model is generating 94.6% back signals, not lay signals.** Of 37 signals produced across 7 runs, 35 are BACK and 2 are LAY. This is not a one-run artefact — it spans every brief file for the day. The lay-only strategy the project is operating under is therefore filtering almost the entire raw signal output rather than being organically supported by the model's outputs. *Relation to established finding #1:* the one proven-edge side (lays below 65% implied) produced just 1 signal all day (the 65–89.99% band lay); the model is structurally oriented toward backing, not laying, which means the post-filter signal rate for the live strategy will remain low unless the model's underlying direction changes. Confidence: high on the observation itself; speculative on cause (the back-skew is consistent with prior documented findings about the Wang transform and calibration bands, but this day's data alone cannot distinguish between them).

2. **One of only two lay signals is in the ≥90% implied-probability band — the project's single most-documented losing segment.** The data shows `by_lay_market_prob_band: {"65-89.99%": {n:1}, ">=90%": {n:1}}`. *Relation to established finding #2:* the ≥90% band has a historical record of 1W/24L. Whether the bot's floor-suppression logic successfully blocked this signal from reaching the operator is not determinable from this data alone — the slip counts show 7 signals sent to slip across the day, but signal-to-slip attrition is expected and the breakdown by band is not available at slip level. It is worth verifying explicitly that this signal was suppressed before reaching the operator. n=1 for the day's lay signals at this level; confidence: speculative on outcome, but the presence of the signal in the generator output at all is the thing worth checking, regardless of n.

3. **Research is running on 97.3% cached data, with zero live Tavily credits consumed.** 292 of 300 markets researched returned cache hits; 0 Tavily credits were used. *Relation to established finding #4:* the measured contribution of web research to signal edge is already ~0.13pp on average. If nearly all "research" is recycled cached content rather than freshly fetched information about today's markets, even that marginal contribution is likely overstated for this day — a cached brief about a cricket match from days ago is not the same information as a fresh one. This is not necessarily a bug (caching is intentional and reduces cost), but it does mean the research step is functioning as a cache-retrieval layer today, not an information-gathering one. The 22 Gemini 429 rate-limit errors and 2 fallback activations indicate API pressure may be reinforcing cache reliance. Confidence: medium — the cache behaviour is confirmed by the numbers; whether cache content was stale is not determinable from this data.

4. **CLV is negative for backs (−0.54%, n=3) and flat for lays (0.0%, n=1).** Closing-line value is an indicator of whether the bot is entering at better or worse prices than where the market ultimately prices the event. A mean CLV of −0.54% on backs means the bot's back entries are, on this day's tiny sample, worse than the closing price — the market moved against the bot's position after entry. *Relation to established finding #3:* the established finding is that the model is "statistically indistinguishable from market pricing." A negative CLV is directionally worse than indistinguishable; it suggests the back entries are at prices that the market then shortens, which is the pattern you'd see if the bot is backing what the market is also moving to back (i.e. following rather than anticipating). n=3 backs, n=1 lays — confidence: speculative; a day's CLV readings at this sample size carry enormous variance.

5. **Consecutive-losses count held at 4 across all 7 runs; no improvement through the day.** The `consec_losses: 4` warning appeared in all 7 run logs, meaning the live book ended the day with the same 4-loss streak it started with — no settlement events during the day moved it. The 3 resolutions that did occur (2 backs, 1 lay) resolved during the day (per the resolutions section), yet the consecutive-loss counter did not reset — indicating the 2 back wins were probe/tracking-only trades, not live positions counting toward the streak. This is consistent with the day's 34/37 back_probe_signals figure: nearly all signals are tracking-only, so wins in that pool do not affect the live streak counter. The live streak of 4 losses may itself be a small sample (n=4), but it is persistent and worth watching if it extends. Confidence: medium on the observation; speculative on whether the streak is statistically meaningful.

## Recommended actions

1. **Verify that the ≥90% implied-probability lay signal was suppressed before reaching the operator's slip.** The signal appears in the raw analyzer output (n=1 in the ≥90% band). If the configured lay-odds floor is working correctly, it should not have appeared on the Telegram slip; if it did, that is a gap between generation-level suppression and slip-level filtering. This is worth one explicit check (e.g. compare the slip log for 2026-09-02 against this signal's market ID) rather than a code change at this stage. The historical 1W/24L record in this band makes it the one segment where a false-pass is most consequential.

2. **Flag for investigation once the cache-hit rate consistently exceeds ~90%: are cached briefs being reused across multiple days for the same open positions?** Today's 97.3% cache rate combined with the known ~0.13pp research contribution means the research pipeline is, on this day, contributing essentially nothing new to signal quality. No immediate action is warranted — caching is correct behaviour and cost-effective — but if the rate stays near 100% across multiple consecutive days, it is worth checking whether briefs are expiring and refreshing on a schedule appropriate for the markets being monitored (e.g. a cricket match brief cached 48 hours ago may be less relevant than it appears). Keep monitoring.

## Caveats

All resolution and CLV figures for this day are based on n=3 settled trades (n=4 CLV-logged positions). At these sample sizes, individual outcomes dominate any statistic: the 66.7% WR, the 100% back WR, and the −0.54% back CLV each carry confidence intervals wide enough to be consistent with almost any underlying true rate. No threshold, model parameter, or strategy decision should be adjusted on the basis of a single day's resolution data.

The consecutive-losses streak of 4 is a live running count, not a fresh sample — the underlying trades occurred over multiple prior days and are not fully characterised by this data.

The 97.3% cache rate is a single-day observation. Whether this rate is typical or elevated by the day's Gemini 429 pressure (22 rate-limit hits) cannot be determined without a baseline from prior days.

For any of the CLV or P&L signals here to become actionable, a minimum of 30 resolved trades in each sub-segment (by side and market-probability band) would be needed to distinguish a meaningful pattern from noise. The back-probe pool is accumulating toward that threshold but is not there yet on a per-segment basis.
